\begin{abstract}
\noindent
PESAD is a rule-based expert system, written in SWI-Prolog, that supports the
diagnosis of the disorders of four chapters of the DSM-5-TR: the
neurodevelopmental, the anxiety, the obsessive-compulsive and related, and the
trauma- and stressor-related disorders. Its knowledge base encodes the criteria
of the manual as rules over four inference levels, from the codable diagnoses,
which include their specifiers and carry their ICD-10-CM and ICD-11 codes, down
to the answers of the clinician; the exclusion criteria are premises required
to be false. The system reasons under uncertainty with certainty factors, i.e.,
with the minimum t-norm, or with one of five alternative methods based on the
product, \L{}ukasiewicz, Hamacher and Einstein t-norms and on graded membership
functions; it investigates all the goals or checks a single one, and it
explains why a question is posed and how a conclusion was reached. The same
engine serves a terminal shell, a Telegram bot and a stateless web API, which
also evaluates the knowledge base from test results, returning lower and upper
certainty bounds and the data still missing. A validation suite of clinical
vignettes gives every goal a typical, a subthreshold, an exclusion and a
differential case, plus comorbidity, specifier, age-rule and uncertainty
cases, and runs them under all the methods; a mutation test measures how many
errors injected into the knowledge base the suite detects. Yet, the vignettes
are written from the same reading of the criteria as the rules, and the
agreement of the system with clinicians on real cases remains to be assessed.
\end{abstract}
