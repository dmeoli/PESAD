\section{Conceptualization}
\label{sec:conceptualization}

We now describe how the knowledge identified above was expressed in logical
form, i.e., the objects of the domain and the relations among them. The
knowledge is organized over four inference levels, which descend from the
codable diagnoses to the elementary answers:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Roman*)]
    \item \emph{disorders}, i.e., the 92 diagnostic goals of
    table~\ref{tab:disorders}, encoded by the relation \code{disorder/3};
    \item \emph{sub-disorders and types}, i.e., the criterion sets of the
    parent (non-codable) disorders, together with the shared exclusions on
    substances and medical conditions, encoded by \code{disorder\_nc/4}, and the
    stimulus types of specific phobia, encoded by
    \code{specific\_phobia\_type/4};
    \item \emph{symptomatic manifestations and features}, i.e., the lettered
    criteria of the manual (a cluster of symptoms with its threshold, a
    duration, an onset, an exclusion), encoded by
    \code{symptomatic\_manifestation/4} and \code{symptomatic\_feature/4};
    \item \emph{elementary facts}, i.e., the answers of the user about single
    items and the choices among options, obtained through the question
    protocols of section~\ref{sec:acquisition}.
\end{enumerate}
The knowledge base has 92 rules at level~I, 37 at level~II and 74 at
level~III (55 manifestations and 19 features, one of which covers the 63
criteria answered by a single question), and the leaves are 238 yes/no items
and 16 questions with a list of options (multiple-choice questions and selective
specifiers, the age band excluded).
